Frequently Asked Questions.

Essential answers regarding foreign investment licensing, corporate structuring, and operational requirements in Saudi Arabia.

Yes. Under the Ministry of Investment (MISA) regulatory framework and the updated Saudi Investment Law, foreign investors and corporate entities can own up to 100% of legal entities across most commercial, industrial, consulting, and technology activities without requiring a local Saudi partner or sponsor. Only a very restricted negative list (e.g. upstream oil exploration and select military manufacturing) remains restricted.
For standard commercial and professional services Limited Liability Companies (LLCs), the Saudi Companies Law no longer mandates an arbitrary statutory minimum capital requirement (such as the former SAR 500,000 threshold). Capital can be determined in the Articles of Association proportionate to the operational plan. However, specific regulated sectors—such as trading licenses (SAR 30M commitment or SAR 26.7M with Saudi partner) or financial services—maintain specific capital thresholds.
Foreign-owned companies are subject to a flat 20% Corporate Income Tax on net adjusted profits attributable to foreign shareholders. Value Added Tax (VAT) is standard at 15%. There is zero personal income tax on salaries. Companies operating under the Regional Headquarters (RHQ) program or within Special Economic Zones benefit from statutory tax holidays of up to 30 years.
The overseas parent corporation must supply: (1) A valid Certificate of Commercial Registration / Incorporation, (2) Current Articles of Association, (3) Audited financial balance sheets for the preceding fiscal year, and (4) A Board Resolution approving the Saudi establishment and appointing the General Manager with Power of Attorney. All overseas documents must be attested by the Saudi Embassy in the origin country or through an official Apostille.
No professional, legitimate advisory firm can guarantee ministerial approvals, as statutory authority rests solely with MISA, the Ministry of Commerce, and regulatory authorities.
