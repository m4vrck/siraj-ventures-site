Yes. Under the Ministry of Investment of Saudi Arabia (MISA) Foreign Investment regulations, 100% foreign ownership is permissible across the majority of commercial, technical, and service activities. Certain specialized activities (such as upstream oil & gas exploration, specific military procurement, and selected restricted retail areas) remain subject to national ownership criteria or special ministry approvals.

For comprehensive details on qualifying activities, consult our Foreign Company Branch Setup guide.

Foreign companies establishing a branch or subsidiary must prepare:

  • A valid Certificate of Incorporation or Commercial Registration from the home jurisdiction
  • Articles of Association and Bylaws of the parent corporation
  • Audited financial statements for the most recent fiscal year
  • A formal Board Resolution authorizing Saudi expansion and appointing the General Manager
  • Power of Attorney attested by the Saudi Embassy and Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA)

All foreign-language documents require certified legal translation into Arabic.

Yes, expatriate residents currently holding valid residency in Saudi Arabia can establish commercial businesses under specific legal frameworks. Depending on the intended commercial activity (classified under Saudi ISIC4 codes), options include forming a Limited Liability Company (LLC) under foreign investment licensing or partnering with Saudi shareholders.

Eligibility depends on current sponsorship status, capital deposit verification, and sector licensing. Siraj Ventures evaluates your specific residency profile to ensure complete compliance.

Under the updated Saudi Companies Law, standard Limited Liability Companies (LLCs) generally have no statutory minimum share capital requirement set by law, allowing partners to determine capital sufficient for their objectives.

However, for foreign-licensed entities, MISA establishes activity-dependent capital standards (e.g., standard service licenses require lower initial capitalization than heavy trading or manufacturing licenses). Siraj Ventures reviews your chosen ISIC4 activity codes to identify exact capital expectations.

MISA (Ministry of Investment) is the regulatory authority responsible for evaluating foreign investment applications and issuing the Foreign Investment License to non-Saudi enterprises.

The Saudi Business Center (SBC) is the Kingdom's unified executive platform that consolidates registrations across MISA, Ministry of Commerce, ZATCA, Chamber of Commerce, and labor entities. Once MISA grants approval, SBC accelerates the unified commercial registration process.

Timelines depend on document readiness and foreign attestation. Once corporate resolutions and attestation packages are complete:

  • MISA Investment License issuance: typically 3 to 7 working days
  • Ministry of Commerce Commercial Registration (CR): 2 to 4 working days
  • Corporate bank account opening & compliance review: typically 2 to 4 weeks depending on the selected bank

Siraj Ventures organizes your filing sequence to minimize dead time between registration milestones.

Every registered Saudi commercial entity must enroll with the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA):

  • Corporate Income Tax: 20% on net profits attributable to non-Saudi foreign ownership share.
  • Value Added Tax (VAT): Mandatory registration at 15% standard rate once annual qualifying taxable revenue meets threshold requirements.
  • Zakat: Applicable only to Saudi and GCC national shares (2.5% calculated on qualifying zakat base).

Siraj Ventures coordinates your post-registration tax onboarding with certified compliance partners.

Corporate bank accounts require the issued Commercial Registration (CR), Chamber of Commerce membership, company stamp, and verified General Manager Iqama or foreign investor identification.

Saudi banks (such as SAB, Al Rajhi, SNB, and Riyad Bank) conduct comprehensive Know Your Customer (KYC) checks on Ultimate Beneficial Owners (UBO). We guide clients on preparing compliant corporate KYC documentation to avoid onboarding delays.
